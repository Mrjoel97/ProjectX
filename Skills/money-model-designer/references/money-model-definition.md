# The Money Model — definition, tiers, stages, rules

## Definition
A **Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers** — *what* you offer, *when* you
offer it, and *how* you offer it, to make as much money as you can as fast as you can.
It is not one product; it is the ordered set of offers a customer moves through.

## The 3 success tiers
1. **Money Model (baseline):** one customer produces more profit than the cost to
   get + service **that one customer** in the first 30 days.
2. **$100M Money Model:** one customer produces more profit than the cost to get +
   service **many** customers in 30 days.
3. **The target:** make enough from one customer to get + service **at least 2 more**
   in under 30 days.

## The 30-day payback rule ("get paid to get customers")
Cover the cost of getting (and servicing) a customer **within 30 days**. The mechanism
is the interest-free ~30-day float on a credit card: spend on ads, get the cash back
inside the billing cycle, pay the card, and reuse the same dollars to get the next
customer — no cash constraint.

Two options for any business:
- **#1 Wait two years and pray** — cash trapped in slow lifetime value.
- **#2 Get paid fast and grow freely** — a good Money Model *is* option #2.

Gym illustration: $5/lead, 20 leads/day, 50% show, 50% buy a $600 core -> ~25% of
leads become customers, plus ~$80 of supplements -> **"$680 per customer before you
open."** Reframed: **"$1 in -> $34 out in 48 hours."**

The metric definitions (30-day cash, cost to get + service) are canonical in
`../../growth-os/references/financial-spine.md`. This skill does not redefine them.

## The 3 stages (evolution of a Money Model)
- **Stage I — Get Cash:** the **Attraction** offer covers acquisition cost.
- **Stage II — Get More Cash:** **Upsell + Downsell** push 30-day profit above cost.
- **Stage III — Get The Most Cash:** **Continuity** maximizes long-term value.

## Evolution order (build in this sequence)
1. Get customers reliably.
2. Customers pay for themselves.
3. Customers pay for **other** customers.
4. Maximize long-term value.
5. Spend as much on ads as possible.

## The 7 governing rules
1. All businesses have money models (whether designed or not).
2. Some money models fit some businesses better than others.
3. Refund if asked.
4. Hard selling is for weak products.
5. Obey the law (especially around the word "free").
6. Be transparent.
7. Any offer, any order (mix and match freely).

## The compounding lever
Value x Volume x Speed multiply, they don't add. Double all three -> the business
grows **8x** (2^3). Triple all three -> **27x** (3^3). Find the lever currently lowest
and raise it.
