# Market Selection — "A Starving Crowd"

You cannot out-offer a bad market. The single biggest determinant of success is **who** you sell to, not what you sell or how well you pitch. Find a starving crowd first.

## Prerequisite: one of the three eternal markets

Every durable market is a subset of one of three eternal human desires:

- **Health**
- **Wealth**
- **Relationships**

The market must be at least **"normal"** — growing roughly at the marketplace rate. **Never enter a shrinking market**, no matter how good your offer is. A tailwind covers many mistakes; a headwind erases many strengths.

## The four indicators of a great market

Rate a candidate market against all four:

1. **Massive Pain** — they "must not *want*, but desperately *need*." Pain is proportional to price: the more it hurts, the more they'll pay. **"The pain is the pitch"** — you don't create desire, you channel existing desperation.
2. **Purchasing Power** — they can actually afford to pay. A market in agony with no money is a charity, not a business.
3. **Easy to Target** — reachable channels exist: associations, email lists, groups, communities, subreddits, conferences. If you can't find them cheaply, you can't sell to them.
4. **Growing** — a tailwind. The market is expanding, not contracting.

## Order of importance — the three levers

```
Starving Crowd (Market)  >  Offer Strength  >  Persuasion Skills
```

Rate each lever **great / normal / bad**.
- A higher-order "great" **overpowers** a lower-order weakness (a great market forgives an average offer).
- A "bad" rating at **any** level stops the whole equation.
- **You cannot be in a "bad" market** — fix that before touching the offer. This is the gate `offer-architect` checks before building: if the market is bad, no offer work will pay off.

## Commit to a niche

Pick **ONE** market and commit. If the business is under ~$10M/yr, **niche down** — the riches are in the niches. Specificity raises both perceived value and price.

### The niching price multiplier (worked example)

The *same* underlying skill commands a higher price as it narrows:

| Positioning | Price |
|---|---|
| "Time Management" | $19 |
| "Time Management **for Sales Professionals**" | $99 |
| "Time Management **for B2B Outbound Sales Reps**" | $499 |
| "Time Management **for B2B Outbound Power Tools & Gardening Sales Reps**" | $1,000–$2,000 |

Hyper-specific beats generic every time. The narrower avatar hears "this is *exactly* for me" and pays a premium for it.

## The positioning statement

Fill this in to lock the niche:

> "I solve **[this type of problem]** for **[this specific type of person]** in this unique, counter-intuitive way that **reverses their deepest fear**."

This becomes the committed artifact of playbook `01-select-market.md` and is recorded to the Scorecard `identity` fields.
