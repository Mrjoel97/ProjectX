# Enhancement Layers — Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees

Once the core offer is built (Value Equation + Stack), four psychological levers multiply its pull. Apply them in order: **Scarcity → Urgency → Bonuses → Guarantee** (then Naming — see `magic-naming.md`).

Two governing principles:
- **The Delicate Dance of Desire** — keep **supply UNDER demand**. Always sell out; publicize the sellout. Fear of loss beats desire for gain.
- **Hormozi's Law** — *"The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make."* Deliver value first; earn the right to a bigger request.

---

## 1. Scarcity (limit quantity → raise value via supply)

Fear of loss > desire for gain. Always sell out and **announce** sellouts.

**3 types of scarcity:**
1. Limited **seats/slots** (physical or logical capacity).
2. Limited **bonuses** (only the first N buyers get the extras).
3. **Never available again** (this offer/version won't return).

**3 service-business implementations (caps):**
1. **Total Business Cap** — "only X clients total." Run a waitlist; when full, raise price 10–20% and re-cap.
2. **Growth Rate Cap** — "only X new clients per week/month."
3. **Cohort Cap** — "only X seats per class/cohort."

**Honest Scarcity** — advertise *real* progress to capacity ("81% to capacity", "3 of 10 spots left"). Never fake it; real scarcity is both ethical and more durable.

---

## 2. Urgency (limit time → lower the action threshold)

"Deadlines drive decisions." Roughly **50–60% of sales land in the last ~4 hours** before a deadline.

**4 methods of urgency:**
1. **Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency** — enrollment closes on a start cadence ("doors close Friday, next cohort in 4 weeks").
2. **Rolling Seasonal Urgency** — the same promo, renamed and re-dated by season ("Spring Special"). **#1 for local businesses.**
3. **Pricing / Bonus-Based Urgency** — the **promo or bonus** expires, not the service itself (price goes up / bonus disappears at the deadline).
4. **Exploding Opportunity** — an external opportunity genuinely decays (a market window, a rule change) and you're naming the clock.

---

## 3. Bonuses (stack instead of discount)

The **same offer broken into stacked bonuses out-pulls the single lumped offer.** Always **add bonuses rather than cut the core price** — discounting trains bargain-hunting; stacking raises perceived value.

**1:1 sales sequence:** ask for the sale **first**. If yes → reveal bonuses after (delight). If no → present the **one bonus that answers their specific objection**, then ask again ("Fair enough?").

**The 11-point Bonus checklist:**
1. **Always** offer a bonus.
2. Give it a **benefit-driven name**.
3. Tell them: (a) how it **relates to their issue**, (b) **what it is**, (c) **how you discovered it**, (d) **how it improves their life**.
4. Offer **proof** (that it works / that it's valuable).
5. Paint a **vivid mental image** of life with it.
6. Attach a **price tag** and justify it.
7. Prefer **tools & checklists** over trainings (faster to value, less effort).
8. Address a **specific obstacle** the buyer faces.
9. Solve the **NEXT logical problem** (before they hit it).
10. Ensure **total bonus value eclipses the core** offer's value.
11. Add **scarcity/urgency to the bonuses** themselves.

**Advanced:** use **Other People's Products** as bonuses — free marketing for them; negotiate a group discount plus a referral commission to you.

---

## 4. Guarantees (reverse the risk)

Risk is the **#1 objection**; reversing it is the fastest lift in the whole offer. Structure every guarantee with **"teeth"**:

> **"If you don't get [X result] in [Y time], we will [Z]."**

**The 4 types:**
1. **Unconditional** — strongest and riskiest; trial-like, no conditions. Best for low-ticket B2C.
2. **Conditional** — payoff tied to the client doing the success actions; framed as *"better than money back."*
3. **Anti-Guarantee** — "all sales final"; requires a creative, believable *"reason why."*
4. **Implied** — performance / revshare / profit-share: *"if I don't perform, I don't get paid."*

**Stacking + the guarantee menu:** combine types, e.g. an unconditional 30-day + a conditional triple-money-back 90-day. Menu of templates to pull from:
No-Questions Refund · Satisfaction-Based · Outsized Refund (double/triple) · Service Guarantee (work free until X) · Modified Service · Credit-Based · Personal Service · Hotel+Airfare · Wage-Payment · Release-of-Service · Delayed Second Payment · First Outcome · Anti-Guarantee · Performance/Revshare/Profit-Share (e.g. revshare 10% top-line / 20% profit / 25% of growth; ratchets 10/20/30%).

**Create your own:** list the buyer's biggest fears → reverse each into a guarantee → **name it vividly**.

**The refund math (does a guarantee pay off?):** a guarantee lifts gross sales but also lifts refunds. Compute **net units both ways**:
```
net_sales = gross_sales x (1 - refund_pct)
```
Book example: baseline **100 sales at 5% = 95 net**; with guarantee **130 sales at 10% = 117 net → 1.23x** (a 23% lift). A guarantee is only *not* worth it if the added refunds fully offset the added sales — rare in practice. Compute it with `scripts/guarantee_refund_calc.py`.
