# Pricing — the Price-to-Value Discrepancy and the Virtuous Cycle

Price is not a number you defend; it is a lever that changes everything downstream — client quality, results, margin, and your own conviction.

## The price-to-value discrepancy

- People buy when **perceived VALUE > PRICE**. The bigger that gap, the easier the sale.
- The discrepancy — not a low number — is what makes an offer feel like a steal. Hormozi's frame: sell **"money at a discount"** — e.g. "$100,000 of value for $10,000."
- **Only raise price after you have raised value.** Price hikes without value increases are just greed and they don't hold.
- **Charge far more than your fulfillment cost** — the gap can be up to **100x** cost. Cost sets a floor, never the price. Value sets the price.

## The Virtuous Cycle of Price

Price is a flywheel that spins in whichever direction you push it.

**RAISE price →**
increases emotional investment → increases perceived value → increases actual results (they show up, do the work) → attracts your **best** clients → **multiplies margin** → funds better fulfillment → justifies higher price. The cycle compounds upward.

**LOWER price →**
decreases emotional investment → decreases perceived value → decreases results → attracts your **worst** clients (bargain hunters, refund-seekers) → **destroys margin** → starves fulfillment → forces price down further. The cycle spirals down.

### Why premium wins
- **"Those who pay the most, pay the most attention."** Paying customers who paid a lot actually implement, so they get results, so they refer and renew.
- Price so it **"stings a little"** — enough that the buyer is emotionally committed.
- Dan Kennedy: *"There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest... but there is for being the most expensive."*

## The anti-pattern — "broke pricing"

Do **not** price like this:
1. Look at the market.
2. Average the competitors.
3. Set your price slightly **below** the average.
4. Try to justify it by offering "a little more."

This is **"more for less"** — a race to the bottom that guarantees thin margins, bad clients, and no room to over-deliver. A Grand Slam Offer does the opposite: charge a **premium**, then stack so much value the price still looks like a bargain.
