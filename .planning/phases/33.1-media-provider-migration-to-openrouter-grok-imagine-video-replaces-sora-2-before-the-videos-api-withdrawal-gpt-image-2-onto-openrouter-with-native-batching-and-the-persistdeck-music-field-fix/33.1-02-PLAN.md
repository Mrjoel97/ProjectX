**The comparison the owner decided on** (PRD §L1, rates read 2026-08-30):

| | `x-ai/grok-imagine-video` | `veo-3.1-lite` (ADR-026) | `sora-2` (today) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Durations | **1-15, any integer** | 4, 6, 8 | 4, 8, 12 |
| 720p | $0.07/s | $0.05/s w/audio, $0.03 w/o | $0.10/s |
| 480p | $0.05/s | not offered | not offered |
| 9:16 | yes | yes | yes |
| Native audio | none | yes | — |

**Cost 1 — the counterparty.** ADR-026 §"1. It introduces NO new data-transfer counterparty — the
decisive point" argues that every other shortlist candidate means a new vendor relationship and a
fresh decision about where customer prompts go, and that Veo needed neither because ADR-016 had
already admitted Google in principle on 2026-08-07. Grok reverses precisely that. xAI becomes a new
recipient of customer scene prompts. OpenRouter is NOT a new counterparty — the text plane has run
on it since `845f4b1` — but xAI is, and routing through OpenRouter does not make the prompt stop
reaching xAI.

**Cost 2 — the structural guarantee that dies with the grid. Not in the PRD; derived during
planning and confirmed against the code.** `packages/cost/src/media.test.ts` currently asserts *"NOT
ONE target duration is reachable with generated video alone"*, and `media.fixtures.json` records
`targetsUnreachableByGeneratedVideoAlone: [15, 30, 60]`. The reason is arithmetic: every member of
sora-2's `4, 8, 12` grid is a multiple of four, so 15 and 30 cannot be composed at all, and 60 is
composable but costs `60 x $0.10 = $6.00`, over `MEDIA_JOB_CAP_USD` of `$3.50`.

Under a `1..15` grid, every integer target is composable. At `$0.07/s`:
- 15 s all-generated = **$1.05** — under the cap. Reachable.
- 30 s all-generated = **$2.10** — under the cap. Reachable.
- 60 s all-generated = **$4.20** — over the cap. Still unreachable.

Left alone, `targetsUnreachableByGeneratedVideoAlone` would collapse from `[15, 30, 60]` to
`[60]`. It does NOT collapse, because of the mitigation below — which is itself a decision this
ADR has to record.
`GENERATED_CLIP_SECONDS`'s own doc comment (`packages/core/src/storyboard.ts:538-545`) calls the
multiple-of-four property *"the real reason the other three kinds exist"*, and
`media-director.md` teaches *"Every legal reel therefore mixes kinds"*. Both become false for 15 s
and 30 s. The kind-mixing discipline stops being STRUCTURAL and becomes purely ECONOMIC — a 30 s
all-generated reel is now merely 7x more expensive than the mixed one, not impossible. The only
remaining defence would be the skill body's "THE ORDER TO REACH IN" prose, and `dispatch.ts` already
records what a prose mandate on this skill family is worth: *"violated twice in six attempts, which
is why this is code and not another sentence in the body."*

**THE MITIGATION — owner decision, 2026-08-30 (`33.1-CONTEXT.md`, "The generated-seconds cap").**
Keep the full `1..15` grid and restore the ceiling ECONOMICALLY, in code:
`MEDIA_GENERATED_SECONDS_CAP = 12`, a cap on TOTAL generated seconds per reservation, enforced
inside `chooseMediaBatch` — the single choke point every reel reservation and every on-screen
estimate already passes through — and surfaced as a governed refusal code
(`over_generated_seconds`), never a silent trim. Plan 33.1-04 Task 4 lands it.

Why 12, and why it works: `min(TARGET_DURATIONS)` is 15, so `12 < 15` refuses an all-generated reel
at EVERY target rather than only at 60 — which is why
`targetsUnreachableByGeneratedVideoAlone` stays `[15, 30, 60]` instead of collapsing. It is the
longest single clip the old Sora grid allowed, and it is exactly what `media-director.md`'s shipped
VARIATION A (4 s + 8 s) and `media.fixtures.json`'s `reel30s.mixed` (3 x 4 s) already spend, so
neither has to be edited. It bounds generated spend at `/usr/bin/bash.84` per reel at any target duration.

**Rejected, with reasons, so a reversal starts from a comparison:** narrowing the grid below what
Grok supports throws away exactly what the new counterparty was accepted for and re-introduces
`illegal_generated_duration`; lowering `MEDIA_JOB_CAP_USD` below .10 shrinks every legitimate
mixed reel's headroom and contradicts ADR-026's own rule about a successor needing a cap change;
relying on the skill body is the prose option the sentence above already prices.

**And the residual, which the ADR must not let the mitigation hide:** the old guarantee could not be
switched off — no sum of 4, 8 and 12 is 15 or 30, and no constant, config or future edit could make
one. The new one is a number somebody can raise in a check somebody can move. Plan 33.1-04 already
found one route around it (a legacy BLOCK deck reaches the same reservation through a different
parser) and resolves it deliberately. That gap between "impossible" and "refused" is the part of
this cost that survives the mitigation.

**Rejections, both on rules rather than taste:**
- `bytedance/seedance-*` — cheapest published per-second rate on OpenRouter's collections page
  ($0.01345/s) and unusable: the live SKU is `{"video_tokens": "0.0000035"}`, billed per video token
  and configuration-dependent, so it is not pre-computable before a request exists.
  `packages/cost/src/media.ts` rule 1 refuses it by construction. This session and ADR-026 reached
  that conclusion independently, which is itself worth recording.
- `x-ai/grok-imagine-image-2.0` — `n: {min: 1, max: 1}`, no batch, and $0.04/image against
  gpt-image-2's ~$0.01. Batch is an explicit owner requirement; a single-vendor story does not
  outrank it. **This is why the image half stays on `gpt-image-2`** and ADR-024's image half is
  untouched.

**What does NOT move:** `MEDIA_JOB_CAP_USD` stays `3.50` (Grok is cheaper than the Sora it
replaces, so ADR-016's never-landed raise to `7.50` stays unlanded). `RUNWAY_DAYS` stays `14` — it
measures a real deadline. `wan2.5-*` rows stay priceable, historical-only, and are not a fallback.

**House style, from `docs/README.md` and ADR-026:** immutable once accepted; supersede, never edit.
Explicit `Supersedes` / `Does NOT supersede` lines at the top. A `Consequences` section stating what
the ADR does NOT itself perform. A `Provenance of this decision` section naming who decided and how
to reverse it. ADR-026's own structure is the template — match it.
