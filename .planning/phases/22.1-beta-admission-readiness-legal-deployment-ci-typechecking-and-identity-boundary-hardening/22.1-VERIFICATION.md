# Phase 22.1 — Goal Verification

**Verdict:** PASSED. Every user-exercisable data and connection control the published privacy policy
promises now exists in the product and does what the policy says. Disconnection, export and erasure
are all implemented, and the one control the product *cannot* perform — Microsoft provider-side
revocation — is stated as a limit rather than implied as a capability, with the user routed to
Microsoft's own consent surface. The audit-immutability invariant that erasure most threatened was
proven against the live production deployment, not against fixtures.

`privacy/page.tsx:312` was found promising an in-app Google disconnect with no implementation
anywhere in the repo. The goal is agreement between what the policy says and what the product does.

## What this phase found that no test could

Two defects reached production behaviour while the suite was green. Both are recorded because the
*mechanism* recurs, not because the bugs were interesting:

1. **`authorizeTenantDeletion` required the deployment-owner grant.** Every real signup received
   `OWNER_REQUIRED` (production requests `9a23216e3f16ebe8`, `431036cd611b309a`, both
   `databaseWriteBytes: 0`). The suite was 6/6 green because *every* fixture seeded `owner: true`,
   making the clause unreachable. An operator-privilege question and a right-over-my-own-data
   question had been given one predicate.
2. **A false claim in source and in the UI.** `disconnectMicrosoft`'s comment asserted the revocation
   endpoint did not exist. Two mechanisms do exist and are deliberately refused, for reasons no test
   can check. The privacy policy meanwhile told Google users where to revoke and said nothing at all
   to Microsoft users — silence that could only read as parity.

Both are now covered by regression guards: a non-owner erasure case that omits `owner` entirely, and
a source-guard asserting both the card and the policy carry Microsoft's consent links and the
explicit "cannot revoke" sentence.
