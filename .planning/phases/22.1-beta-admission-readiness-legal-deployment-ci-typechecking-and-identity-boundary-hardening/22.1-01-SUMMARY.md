# 22.1-01 Summary — Disconnect Google (revoke, then delete)

A published legal claim became true. `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx:312` has stated since launch
that "you can disconnect your Google account at any time from within the application" — and
offers that as a distinct alternative to `myaccount.google.com/permissions`, so it is a promise
about an in-app control, not a signpost to Google. No such control existed. A repo-wide grep
found zero calls to any revoke endpoint and no token-delete mutation outside `store`'s
reconnect delete-then-insert.

## The three decisions worth not relitigating

**It lives in `gmailAuth.ts`, not `gmail.ts`.** `llmRedaction.test.ts:154-169` statically pins
gmail.ts's POST set to exactly `[TOKEN_ENDPOINT, SEND_ENDPOINT]` and asserts the total
`method: "POST"` count equals that list's length. A third POST there fails by construction, and
"just update the test" would delete a real governance guard — it exists so no new write verb
reaches Google ungoverned. The default (non-node) Convex runtime has `fetch`; `voiceToken.ts`
and `http.ts`'s code exchange already depend on that.

**It POSTs the REFRESH token, not the access token.** Google accepts either, but revoking an
access token expires one short-lived credential and leaves the grant — and the user's
"Third-party access" entry — standing. That is precisely the bug this plan closes, and a naive
200-check would pass it. The test asserts the body is literally `token=refresh-token`.

**200 and 400 both mean revoked, and the local delete runs unconditionally.** A 400 is Google
saying the token is already invalid — the same end state (the `eventIdFor` 409-is-success
discipline). Ordering is load-bearing in both directions: delete-only leaves the grant live on
the user's Google account; revoke-only leaves the crown-jewel refresh token at rest in a DB
where nothing honours it.

## Deliberate non-goals (recorded in cockpit.md's Known gaps)

A disconnect strands rows on purpose: requests held at `awaiting_reauth`, unread
`gmail_reconnect` notifications, and armed future-`sendAt` schedulers. Each degrades to a
non-throwing hold, and the expiry cron creates no new notifications once the row is gone, so the
leak is bounded to what already exists. Cleaning them up means building the reconnect-RESUME
sweep — which does not exist today either, despite `connect-gmail/page.tsx`'s comment claiming
reconnect "resumes any awaiting_reauth delivery". **That resume is the real missing feature**;
this plan adds no new breakage.

Also untouched and still red: `apps/web/e2e/connect-gmail.spec.ts` asserts a "Connect Gmail"
heading, but the page has said "Connect Google" since the 17-02 calendar widening.

## Requirement — GOVN-03 (minted 2026-08-01)

*User-exercisable data and connection controls match the published privacy policy.* Until this
was minted, Phase 22.1 carried no requirement id at all, breaking the milestone's 1:1
requirement-to-phase map; GOVN-01 (Phase 22, owner authorization) and GOVN-02 (Phase 24, ISO
conformance) neither covered it.

The framing that makes it useful rather than decorative: **the published privacy policy is the
specification.** This plan existed because `privacy/page.tsx:312` promised an in-app disconnect
that had no implementation anywhere in the repo — a documentation-vs-implementation gap no test
could catch, because there was nothing to test. GOVN-03 makes that class of gap a tracked
requirement rather than something spotted by luck.

It also gives **tenant data deletion and export** a home — unowned GDPR Art. 17 work flagged in
`.planning/design/growth-surfaces-canvas-funnels-connections.md` §5.10, and NOT yet built. That
keeps GOVN-03 open after this plan closes.
