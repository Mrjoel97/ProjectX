## Implementation

New staged films persist native mode; old plans without the field retain their original audio
contract. Native30/45/60s storyboards contain only standard Seedance2.0 generated scenes,
4–15s each, exactly summing to the selected length. The prompt envelope binds picture, exact
spoken words, overlays and sound intent into the reviewed purchase. Native acquisitions omit
separate TTS, stock music, stock B-roll and still-image generation. Old jobs, receipts, credits
and artifact readers remain supported.

Native admission permits up to60 requested generated seconds/$15 policy estimate per film,
separately from legacy12s/$3.50. Exact review and tenant/deployment budgets still apply. Per-clip
standard720p allowance remains `0.1512 × (seconds + 1)`; this is not a guaranteed invoice ceiling.
Longer films cost materially more than the old mixed workflow. No additional paid request is
authorised by changing source admission; the original testing allowance remains$5.

The assembler preserves native scene sound from scene start, validates coverage/decode and
normalises final delivery. It extracts the actual assembled soundtrack in the existing sandbox
for transcription. Audio bytes/hash, batch and source-film identity bind the checked caption
path; actual timestamps remain on the film clock. Transcript/script mismatch refuses checked
captions and leaves the uncaptioned film available. New storage references participate in
tenant ownership/export/erasure; resetting native sources deletes the old extracted PCM only.

The workspace shows three stages: Picture & sound, Assemble, Captions. Native length choices
are30/45/60. Failed native scenes offer explicit paid regeneration; stock/still downgrade arms
remain available only to historical workflows. Native estimates explain their policy allowance.

## Keeping quality consistent

Director v9 grounds the brief in the user's product information, plans intentional scene
progression and varies product angles, framing, motion and motivated lighting. Golden hour,
studio gradients, rim light and macro detail are choices serving the product and story. Each
scene repeats identity and sound direction instead of assuming independent clips share context.
The director must not invent unseen product details or unsupported claims.

Technical gates can reject broken files, absent/short audio, unsupported purchases and incorrect
checked captions. They do not prove beautiful imagery, matching voices or product fidelity.
The approved sample is one4s fictional unbranded product. Full-film multi-shot/voice continuity
and genuine product-reference image input still need qualification. Written/cited references
are not automatically attached provider images. Do not claim an automatic visual quality judge
or that every future film will match this sample.
