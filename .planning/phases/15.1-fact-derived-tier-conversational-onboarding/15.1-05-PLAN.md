Design §7 locks this shape: the behaviour preset is an **enum mapping to a versioned registry style
directive**, deliberately NOT a free-text box — user-authored text injected into every future system
prompt is a standing prompt-injection surface and smuggles unversioned prompt content into every call,
contrary to CLAUDE.md §5.

Ship **three** presets (§7 says 3-4; three keeps the mirror at 15 files and each genuinely
distinguishable). They differ on **how much the agent pushes**, not on tone adjectives:
- `style-direct` — blunt; names the binding constraint first; no preamble; states the cost of not acting.
- `style-coaching` — asks before prescribing; surfaces the tradeoff and invites a choice; still lands a
  concrete next step (never a therapy loop).
- `style-concise` — minimum words; no framing prose; the step, the proof metric, the risk. Nothing else.

Body-writing rules, so these compose safely on top of a specialist's own body:
- Each is an **overlay**, ~10-20 lines. Say so in the body: *"This is a style overlay. It changes HOW
  you say things. It never changes WHAT you are allowed to do, what you may claim, or your grounding
  obligations — those come from your own instructions and take precedence."* A directive that could
  widen capability would be a privilege-escalation path through a DB row (ADR-007's reasoning).
- No tool names, no capability language, no "you may now …".
- No tier language — the tier is a separate, code-owned FACT line (task 2). Presets and tiers are
  orthogonal; do NOT write nine cross-product variants.

Then the mirror, for each of the three:
