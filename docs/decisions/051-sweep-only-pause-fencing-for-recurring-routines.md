# ADR-051 — Sweep-only pause fencing for recurring routines

- **Status:** Accepted as a conditional governance amendment on 2026-09-25; operational recurrence remains deferred.
- **Drafted:** 2026-09-25.
- **Accepted path:** `docs/decisions/051-sweep-only-pause-fencing-for-recurring-routines.md`.
- **Owner route choice:** On 2026-09-25 the owner selected “B — keep sweep; draft narrow D6 amendment (recommended)” after the independently reviewed [47-17 D6 packet](../../.planning/phases/47-the-schedule-row-that-re-arms/47-17-D6-DECISION-PACKET.md). That selected a drafting path, not this text, an implementation, a D6 verdict, or a release.
- **Conditional supersession:** Only the *pending per-routine scheduled-function cancellation* clause and callback-specific wording of [ADR-046 D6](046-a-standing-approval-reads-and-prepares-it-never-sends.md), and only when the sweep-only invariant below is proved. ADR-046 D1–D5 and D7–D9, D6's immediate pause/revoke and authoritative state/version recheck, and [ADR-050](050-recurrence-build-for-evidence.md) otherwise remain operative. ADR-050's literal “pause/cancel” candidate-evidence wording needs a separate exact-text governance reconciliation before anyone may count the candidate's D6 row as passing.

## Context

ADR-046 D6 assumes each routine has a pending scheduled function to cancel on pause. The preserved Phase 47 **future** post-gate design instead computes due-ness from a routine's timezone and last occurrence key through a bounded shared sweep; it stores no next-arm chain or per-routine scheduled-function ID. The production `reliability-sweep` currently handles unrelated watchdog work behind `RELIABILITY_SWEEP_ARMED`; it does **not** select routines. Canceling that shared cron on one tenant's pause would stop unrelated work and would not be a per-routine cancellation. The isolated candidate writes paused state and increments a version, then rechecks status/version at claim and attempt boundaries, but has **no sweep selector** and stores `nextDueUtcMs`. It has no cancel target. That is partial automated design evidence, not literal D6 compliance or proof of the future sweep integration.

## Decision

For a recurring-routine implementation **only if** an independently reviewed source/reachability proof establishes all of the following, ADR-046 D6's best-effort cancellation of a *pending per-routine scheduled function* is replaced by a stronger no-pending-function invariant plus transactional pause fencing:

1. Due routine work is selected by a **bounded shared sweep**. No routine owns a pending scheduled callback, scheduled-function ID, self-arming chain, stored next-arm timestamp, or tenant-callable arm path. The shared sweep is never canceled because one routine pauses. A one-shot unrelated evidence probe, including `dstProbe`, does not become a routine callback by this exception.
2. Pause or revoke atomically records the stopped state and increments a monotonically checked approval/version fence for that routine. A due selector may be stale, but its **claim transaction** must re-read current status, version, due-ness and last occurrence and refuse a claim if the pause or material change committed first. The occurrence claim is atomic and idempotent for a zone-resolved occurrence key, including both readings of a repeated wall hour.
3. If a claim committed first, pause still prevents **later admissions**. A reservation attempt needs one uniquely identified, transactionally fenced admission per run; if pause commits first, admission refuses. If admission commits first, the reservation may settle after pause, but must be reconciled/released on the terminal path without admitting a paid step. Each paid step needs a separate uniquely identified admission that re-reads status/version and run state, with its whole-run reservation already valid. A transactionally enforced **single outstanding paid-step admission per run** prevents two calls from being admitted concurrently: replay of the same step ID is idempotent and cannot call twice, and a retry or next step cannot be admitted until the prior step has landed a closed terminal/retry outcome and the fence is rechecked. Pause and admission must serialize or have an equivalently proved fail-closed interlock: if pause commits first, no paid step is admitted; if one step's admission commits first, that **one outstanding step** counts as already in flight even if its physical network call starts after pause. It may finish, but completion re-reads the fence, cannot create an actionable plan or admit a successor paid step, releases unused reservation and writes only a closed refs-only outcome. A mere check followed by an asynchronous reserve or call is not an admission guarantee. Every queued worker and retry must revalidate before its next admission.
4. A routine-linked proposed plan prepared before pause cannot receive a **new external-action admission** under the old routine approval/version. Every external write still requires a separate per-run human approval under D1, and its action-admission transaction must also revalidate the routine fence or explicitly originate a new, independently governed attended action. If an external action was already admitted before pause, its physical send may complete under the existing governed cancellation/terminal protocol; it cannot be retroactively unsent. Neither standing approval nor this amendment grants that admission or bypasses the human gate.
5. A missed occurrence is skipped after one named grace window, not queued for catch-up. A paused routine has no future due claim; an outage, deploy, overlapping sweeps, retry or DST transition cannot drain stale occurrences into a burst. One active run per routine and closed retry/terminal classes remain mandatory.

The safety property is not “no callback exists, therefore pause is safe.” It is: **after pause/revoke commits, no new claim, reservation admission, paid-step admission, actionable-plan commit or external-action admission may succeed under the old fence**. A step admitted earlier can physically start or finish later, but it must stop at its next fence/terminal boundary; this is the explicit limit of an immediate pause that does not interrupt a call mid-flight. A source scan or a pre-call read alone is insufficient to prove the transaction order.

This conditional amendment does not apply if a later implementation adds any per-routine pending function, ID, self-arm chain or stored next-arm timestamp. The original ADR-046 D6 cancellation obligation then remains unresolved until a new architecture/governance review, exact inventory and tests address it. Renaming a callback or hiding it behind another module does not qualify for the exception.

## Evidence required before D6 can pass

The consuming implementation must demonstrate, with adverse-path automated tests and independent review of actual source/reachability, at least:

- A bounded sweep selector, tenant isolation and transactionally revalidated claim; pause racing due selection and claim in both commit orders; simultaneous sweeps claiming one occurrence; the two fall-back wall-hour readings collapsing onto one key; spring-forward nonexistence; and a stopped routine never re-arming.
- Pause racing a queued attempt, retry, reservation **admission and settlement**, paid-step **admission and physical call start**, in-flight call completion, and plan/action admission. Force pause between a successful final read and each asynchronous reserve/call to prove that a read alone is insufficient. Test both commit orders: pause-first refuses admission and causes no new call for that step; admission-first permits at most the **single outstanding** already-admitted step even if its network start is later, with no successor admission or actionable result. Race two different paid-step IDs within one run against each other and pause; only one may be outstanding, while replay of its same ID must not make another call. Exercise retry after terminal settlement with a fresh fence check. Each branch must show its terminal class, both spend-rail reservation release on every terminal outcome, and refs-only audit/DLQ. The real rail integration must prove admission/settlement atomicity or conservative fail-closed compensation; injected stubs cannot establish it.
- Deployment restart, outage and delayed sweep execution beyond the grace window: skip/no burst/no stale replay, one active run, bounded retries, and no provider-refusal retry. Real rate-limiter integration—not only injected stubs—must prove whole-envelope reservation and idempotent refund/reconciliation.
- Closed import/scheduler inventory showing no public/tenant caller, per-routine scheduled function, self-arm chain or outbound edge in the disabled candidate. The actual later production implementation must be reviewed independently rather than inherited from candidate tests.

The current six-file candidate's nine synthetic tests and stage checker do **not** establish these conditions in production. They test only the isolated design; they do not demonstrate a bounded deployed selector, real spend rails, actual per-run approval integration, or live OAuth/provider/DST behavior. A D6 row remains `missing` until the accepted governance texts are reconciled and the implemented behavior and adverse paths pass substantive review. No test or source citation may be relabeled `live`.

## Other production-design gaps this amendment does not settle

The test-only candidate stores `nextDueUtcMs` and next-local fields instead of computing due-ness from zone and last occurrence key, keeps prompt fields directly instead of referencing `savedPrompts`, and has a separate `candidateDeadLetters` table instead of reusing the existing `deadLetters`. Its rails and proposed-plan approval are stubs. Each difference needs a separate implementation/reconciliation plan and verification. This amendment changes none of those design decisions and does not make the six-file candidate promotable.

## Unchanged evidence and release boundary

ADR-046 D1's per-run human approval for every external write; D2's closed structured material-change list; D3's skip/no burst; D4's one active run; D5's closed retry classes; D7's refs-only audit; D8's up-front whole-run reservation and terminal release; and D9's real fired DST probe evidence all remain in force. `dst-boundary`, `oauth-expiry-reauth` (actual seven-day grant expiry, explicit reconnect and no catch-up burst), and `provider-read` still require three distinct collected real-world traces and human review. The four already-armed DST probe module/export names remain stable through their last fire.

The historical [29 decision](../../.planning/phases/29-unified-knowledge-and-routines/29-RECURRENCE-DECISION.md) stays immutable and operationally `defer`. ADR-050's `build-for-evidence` stage remains isolated, disabled and expiring 2026-12-31. Exact-text acceptance alone did not materialize this ADR or update the checker. This separately reviewed transition materializes the ADR and a hash-pinned metadata exception; it does not update the stage, pass D6, enable a tenant, deploy recurrence, authorize provider/paid calls or sends, or make `enable-safe` eligible. A later bounded ADR/checker/ADR-050 reconciliation, technical proof, all twelve substantive matrix rows, three live artifacts and a separate owner release decision remain required.

## Stop and recovery

If the no-pending-function invariant, pause fence, spend release, approval boundary, isolation, or evidence review fails, leave operational `defer` in force and mark D6 unpassed. Do not cancel the global sweep or claim that removing candidate source rolls back a production schema. Any later accepted-ADR/checker transition must capture its exact-file baseline and restore only those files if validation fails; never leave a recurrence-named accepted ADR in `docs/decisions` while the current defer checker rejects it.
